Pickleball for Beginners: Rules, Gear, Costs

By BlogSphere Mag  ·  November 2026  ·  7 min read

Pickleball is easy to learn and growing fast in the US, UK, and India. Here are the rules, gear you need, and what it costs.

Cover: photorealistic pickleball court entrance. Photo: GA Kevin, Wikimedia Commons, CC0.

Are Pickleball and Tennis the Same Game?

No. Pickleball is a separate sport that uses a solid paddle, a lower-net, and a smaller court than tennis. Players hit underhand, the ball bounces once on each side, and the serving player must stay behind the baseline. It is easier to learn than tennis and popular with beginners of all ages, which is why the game has grown fastest in the United States, the United Kingdom, and India. While it shares some DNA with tennis and badminton, its unique rules make it more accessible and social.

The primary difference lies in the pace and physics. A tennis ball is designed for speed and bounce over a large area, whereas a pickleball is a perforated plastic ball that travels slower and is more susceptible to wind. This means the game is played in a tighter space, requiring more strategic placement and reflex-based volleying rather than raw power. For many, this lower physical barrier makes it the ideal sport for lifelong fitness.

How Is Pickleball Different from Tennis?

Pickleball uses a solid paddle rather than a stringed racquet, and the net is 36 inches at the sidelines and 34 inches in the middle. The ball is solid and quieter than a tennis ball. A key rule is the non-volley zone, or kitchen, a seven-foot strip near the net where players cannot volley. The serve must be underhand and diagonal, and the server scores only when the receiver wins the right to return.

The 'kitchen' is the most distinctive part of the game. It prevents players from simply standing at the net and smashing every ball, forcing a more tactical 'dinking' game where players softly push the ball over the net. This creates a unique tension where players must carefully time their advance into the non-volley zone. Understanding when to dink and when to drive is the mark of a skilled player.

What Equipment Do You Need to Start?

All you need is a paddle, a perforated plastic ball, and a court. Beginners can buy a starter paddle and ball for under ₹300 in India or under $50 in the United States. A beginner paddle is longer and heavier than a table-tennis paddle but lighter than a tennis racquet, which helps beginners control the ball. Most kits include a pack of balls and a small net.

As players progress, they often upgrade to graphite or carbon fiber paddles, which provide better spin and power. Choosing the right paddle depends on whether the player prefers 'touch' (control) or 'pop' (power). Footwear is also critical; since pickleball involves a lot of lateral movement, court shoes with non-marking soles are recommended to prevent ankle injuries and preserve the court surface.

How Much Does Pickleball Cost?

A starter paddle and ball cost roughly $30-$50 in the United States and between ₹1,500 and ₹3,000 in India. Players often rent or borrow equipment before buying, since the learning curve is short. A court can be built or rented; community courts in the United States are free in many parks, and in India, private clubs charge hourly rates that vary by city. Gear costs alone are low, but court access is the main expense.

For those looking to play regularly, joining a local club is the most cost-effective route. Memberships often include access to multiple courts, organized mixers, and coaching sessions. In India, the rise of corporate parks and residential complexes adding pickleball courts has made the sport more accessible to urban professionals, reducing the need for expensive private club memberships.

Why Is Pickleball Growing So Fast?

Pickleball grew fastest in the United States because it is easy to pick up, needs little space, and suits all ages. Walkabouts, a leading equipment brand, said pickleball players in the United States grew by more than 40 percent in 2024, a record. The United Kingdom and India have seen similar growth as schools, clubs, and community centres add courts. The low cost and quick matches make it attractive to beginners and families.

The social nature of the game is its biggest draw. Because the court is small, players are close enough to converse during the match, turning the sport into a community event. It bridges generational gaps, allowing grandparents to play competitively with their grandchildren. This inclusivity has transformed it from a niche hobby into a global phenomenon.

What Are the Basic Rules of Pickleball?

The server serves diagonally from behind the baseline, and the receiver must let the ball bounce before returning it. After the return, either side may volley, except inside the non-volley zone. Each side must let the ball bounce once before volleying, so every rally begins with a double-bounce. A rally ends when the ball hits the ground twice, goes out of bounds, or is hit into the kitchen on the volley. Points are scored by the serving side only.

The double-bounce rule is critical; it prevents the serving team from rushing the net immediately and ending the point too quickly. This ensures that the receiving team has a fair chance to establish their position. Scoring can be confusing for newcomers, as it typically involves three numbers (server score, receiver score, and server number), but once mastered, the flow of the game is rhythmic and fast.

Where Can You Play Pickleball in India?

Indian cities are adding courts quickly. Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, and Hyderabad have community courts in parks and clubs, and private academies offer hourly rentals. Bengaluru's ecological parks host open play sessions, and Delhi's district centres have weekend beginner clubs. Foreign travelers can find courts at resorts and hotel leisure facilities in Goa, Kerala, and the Andaman Islands.

The growth in India is particularly strong in Tier-1 cities where space is at a premium. Pickleball's smaller footprint makes it an attractive alternative to tennis for developers. Many new sports complexes are now designating 'multi-sport' areas where a single tennis court can be converted into four pickleball courts, maximizing the number of players per square foot.

Is Pickleball a Good Workout?

Yes. A one-hour game raises the heart rate, builds leg strength, and improves agility. The average player burns 300-500 calories per hour, depending on intensity and body weight. The sport combines short bursts of sprinting with deliberate footwork, which suits people of many fitness levels. Because it is low-impact, it is gentler on joints than tennis or running.

Beyond calorie burning, the game improves hand-eye coordination and cognitive function. The fast-paced nature of the 'kitchen' rallies requires split-second decision-making and precise motor control. For seniors, it provides a safe way to maintain cardiovascular health and social connection, reducing the risk of isolation and cognitive decline.

What Is the Origin of the Name?

Pickleball was invented in 1965 by a group of fathers on Bainbridge Island, Washington, United States. The name came from a family dog named Pickles, a spotted beagle who belonged to one of the inventors, Joel Pritchard. The dog appeared in early photographs and soon became part of the sport's identity, which is why the logo features a dog.

Another popular story suggests the name comes from 'pickling' the ball, referring to a mixed group of players, similar to a pickle dish. While the dog story is more widely accepted by the founders, both narratives reflect the eclectic and inclusive spirit of the game. Today, the sport's identity continues to evolve, but its roots remain firmly planted in family fun and improvisation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is pickleball easier than tennis?

Yes. The smaller court, lower net, solid paddle, and underhand serve lower the barrier to entry. Most beginners can play a rally within an hour.

Do you need your own court to play?

No. Many parks and clubs have public or hireable courts, and beginners can rent or borrow equipment. Singles matches are less common than doubles, which also keeps the court demand lower.

Is pickleball a good workout?

Yes. It raises the heart rate, builds leg strength, and improves agility, burning roughly 300-500 calories per hour while staying low-impact.

What is the best paddle for a total beginner?

Beginners should look for a composite or graphite paddle with a wide sweet spot. Avoid expensive carbon-fiber pro models until you have developed a consistent swing and understand your preference for power versus control.

Can you play pickleball on a tennis court?

Yes, you can use temporary lines or tape to mark a pickleball court on a tennis court. Since the dimensions are smaller, a single tennis court can actually fit up to four pickleball courts if marked correctly.

How long does a typical match last?

Most games are played to 11 points, and matches are often best-of-three. A single game usually lasts 15-20 minutes, making it a fast-paced activity that fits easily into a busy schedule.
